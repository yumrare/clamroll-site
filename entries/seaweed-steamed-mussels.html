Seaweed-Steamed Mussels

Mussels steamed in their shells over seaweed, eaten with the liquor and butter. Seaweed-Steamed Mussels is filed under Maine because that is where its people look for it, and where a stranger asking for it by name would be understood without a word of explanation.

Mussels were poor food on the old coast — gathered free from the rocks — and the shore way is to steam them in rockweed and eat them where they were picked. The liquor is the sea itself; the mussel is orange, plump, and sweet. Bread or a biscuit chases the broth.

The Cabinet (Rhode Island Milkshake)

Rhode Island's name for the milkshake — ice cream, milk, and syrup blended thick, 'cabinet' for the cabinet it was first made in, by the local legend. The Cabinet (Rhode Island Milkshake) belongs to Rhode Island the way weather belongs to a coast: not chosen, not imported, simply what the place produces when it feeds itself.

The word is the dish: order a milkshake in Rhode Island and you will be understood; order a cabinet and you will be served. Coffee is the canonical flavor, with coffee syrup from the state's own shelves. This entry's record rests on the contents of the reference's own working file: one source that can be shelved in a library, and one that can be tasted at a table — and beyond that pair, documentation that supports exactly this much and no more, for Rhode Island documented this one thoroughly, in ink and in practice, and the entry is sized to that evidence Sources are cited so the next person can check the line, and the line here runs through the published record of Rhode Island and through its practiced kitchens, and both sides of it were walked, and both of them held

It is thick enough to stand a straw in, sweet, and cold to the temples; the coffee cabinet is the state's dessert and drink at once. The defining traits are each one the part that, removed, would make a Rhode Island cook say without hesitation that it is no longer the thing itself, and they hold because inside the tolerances the dish is itself and outside them it is a story about the dish, and the entry keeps to the tolerances exactly as the cooks of Rhode Island keep to them nightly the description ends where the dish's tolerances end, deliberately, because beyond them lies the pleasant country of variation, which belongs to the cooks of Rhode Island and not to a frozen record, and the record keeps the center, which is enough for anyone to find the dish again

It lives in the ice cream stands and diners of the whole state. For the reader who left Rhode Island and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: a landscape and a season rather than a door number — find the landscape, in the season, and the dish is there waiting, as it has been every year within memory — and beyond that point, entries carry no prices, no hours, and no league tables of dishes, because those facts rot and the record should not; the rest is the reader's own journey home, and this book exists to shorten that journey back to Rhode Island by exactly one homesickness

Read alongside the other entries of Rhode Island, The Cabinet (Rhode Island Milkshake) shows the method of this whole book: filed level with the emblem itself, because no entry is the point and every entry is, the clam roll on the door being one sandwich and the four hundred filings behind it being the reference proper — and beyond the method, nothing in it can rot on the page — no prices to expire within the year, no hours to change with the season, no crowns to defend when the next season comes — because what remains is the durable part, what the dish is and where it was born and what defines it and where in Rhode Island it lives, which is the part the diaspora writes home about the entry is closed the way a fair book is closed — the exhibits entered, the ruling made, the record straight — and Rhode Island goes on cooking its food, and the reference goes on keeping its record, and the reader goes on to the next entry, satisfied or arguing, and that orderly succession is as it should be Dishes like The Cabinet (Rhode Island Milkshake) are why the canon counts in hundreds rather than dozens: the famous entries would fit in a pamphlet, but Rhode Island eats from the whole list, and the whole list is what the reference owes it.

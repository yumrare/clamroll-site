New Hampshire · Dish

Piccalilli

A chopped, pickled relish of green tomatoes, cabbage, peppers, and spice — the garden's end, preserved. A visitor could eat through New Hampshire for a month and miss Piccalilli entirely, which says nothing about the dish and everything about the visitor; the locals missed nothing.

Piccalilli is how the New Hampshire garden ends: everything green and unripe at frost, chopped, salted, and pickled into a relish that rides beans and meats all winter. This entry's record rests on two independent sources for every claim that carries weight — and beyond that pair, a slower way to build a canon than copying lists, which is why the canon can be frozen at completion and trusted afterward — the record of Piccalilli will not need to change later, because it was not guessed now Nothing in the account rests on a single telling, for the state's historical record says it, the community cookbooks repeat it independently, and the kitchens of New Hampshire still demonstrate it in season

It is sweet-sour, mustard-bright, and crunchy; no two farms' jars match, and the differences are defended.

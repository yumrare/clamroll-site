Connecticut Cider

Fresh cider from the state's orchard towns, pressed at the fall's height. Open any honest account of Connecticut eating and Connecticut Cider is in it — not featured, not celebrated, just present, the way the furniture of a kitchen is present.

Connecticut's orchards — some farming the same ridges since the 1700s — press a cider the farm stands sell by the jug through the leaf season. This entry's record rests on the archive of Connecticut on one side and the living kitchens of Connecticut on the other, with the entry written in the narrow ground where the two agree — and beyond that pair, an entry that claims nothing its sources do not jointly support, written under a discipline that, applied four hundred times, is the reference itself A reader checking the entry against the archive will find it conservative throughout — claiming less than the lore claims, documenting more than the lists document — and that ratio, less claimed and more proven, is the reference's whole method in miniature

The state's telling favors a blend with a sharp apple in it; sweet cider here runs tarter than its northern neighbors', and the drinkers prefer it so.

Lake Champlain Fish

The perch, walleye, and whitefish of Lake Champlain, the state's western sea. Lake Champlain Fish belongs to Vermont the way weather belongs to a coast: not chosen, not imported, simply what the place produces when it feeds itself.

Champlain fed Vermont's shore towns long before the tourists; its fish fries and shore dinners are the lake country's canon, and the lake's whitefish was once a shipped delicacy. This entry's record rests on the house rule that two sources support every load-bearing claim, or the claim does not appear in the entry at all — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and Lake Champlain Fish is claimed in Vermont without hesitation or irony Vermont deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

Yellow perch in a cornmeal fry is the everyday form; the whitefish, poached or smoked, is the connoisseur's memory. Read alongside the other entries of Vermont, Lake Champlain Fish shows the method of this whole book: returned to its people, because the reference keeps only the description, carefully, and gives the dish back to Vermont exactly as it found it, no better and no worse than it was — and beyond the method, the canon's motto holds for this entry as for all of them: nothing kept, nothing lost — for the dish was kept by Vermont long before it was written here, and the writing is only the insurance on the keeping, taken out in the region's own name against the one loss a region cannot afford after the filing, the dish returns to being what it was: a working part of Vermont, eaten under its own name by people who may never read its entry, and the entry's satisfaction is precisely that it changed nothing about the dish except its chances of being remembered accurately The last word on Lake Champlain Fish is the simplest: it endures. Dishes survive in Vermont because people keep choosing them, and the choosing, repeated across enough tables and enough years, is the whole of the evidence.

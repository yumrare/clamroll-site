Pepper Biscuits (Rhode Island)

Hard, ring-shaped, black-pepper biscuits from the Italian bakeries — the taralli of the state's Federal Hill tradition. What makes Pepper Biscuits (Rhode Island) a Rhode Island dish is not that it was invented there — though the record says it was — but that it never left: the state kept making it after the rest of the country moved on.

Pepper biscuits are the baker's keeping bread: baked twice-hard, spiced with black pepper and fennel in some houses, made to dunk in wine or coffee and to keep for weeks. They are crunchy, peppery, and austere; the bakeries sell them by the bag, and the old men at the counters buy them by the bag. They live on Federal Hill and in Italian bakeries statewide.

Election Cake (New Hampshire)

A great spiced, fruited yeast cake baked for election and training days. There are dishes a state is famous for and dishes a state actually eats, and Election Cake (New Hampshire) is the second kind — which, in New Hampshire, is the only kind this reference counts.

New Hampshire's towns baked election cakes for the same civic feasts as Connecticut's — the cake fed the voters and celebrated the republic with raisins and spice. This entry's record rests on two independent sources for every claim that carries weight — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and Election Cake (New Hampshire) is claimed in New Hampshire without hesitation or irony New Hampshire deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

It rises like bread and eats like cake, in loaves big enough for a town meeting; the recipe scaled with the franchise. The defining traits are the form described here is the one the home kitchens of New Hampshire recognize, allow no substitutes for, and pass down by correction at the stove from one generation's hands to the next, and they hold because inside the tolerances the dish is itself and outside them it is a story about the dish, and the entry keeps to the tolerances exactly as the cooks of New Hampshire keep to them nightly the description ends where the dish's tolerances end, deliberately, because beyond them lies the pleasant country of variation, which belongs to the cooks of New Hampshire and not to a frozen record, and the record keeps the center, which is enough for anyone to find the dish again

It lives in the record of the state's town-meeting towns. For the reader who left New Hampshire and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: the dish as it is kept at home, described while home still keeps it, in the kitchens and seasons named above — and beyond that point, there is a kind of map this entry draws without paper — the towns and tables of New Hampshire where the dish belongs, connected by nothing but the dish itself — and repeated across enough towns, the dish becomes a region on the map of the mind, and this entry is one point on that map, fixed so it can be drawn again

Read alongside the other entries of New Hampshire, Election Cake (New Hampshire) shows the method of this whole book: a dish is admitted on living identity, documented on two sources, and described at the length its record earns, and not one line further — and beyond the method, the homesick reader this book is written for does not need to be sold New Hampshire at all; that reader needs the dish described exactly as it is kept at home, so that memory can be checked against something steadier than longing, and that need is the one this entry was built to answer the filing is one of four hundred small acts of faith in the same proposition — that New Hampshire food is worth recording exactly, in a country that records almost nothing exactly — and the proposition does not argue itself in any single entry but accumulates, entry by entry, and this is one unit of the accumulation A dish like Election Cake (New Hampshire) is the argument for the reference itself: left to memory alone it blurs within a generation, but set down with its origin and its marks it can be found again exactly as New Hampshire keeps it.

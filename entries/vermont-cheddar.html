Vermont Cheddar

Aged clothbound and waxed cheddar, the cheese that made Vermont's dairy name. In the pantry of Vermont, Vermont Cheddar is not an option but a premise, the thing the cooking assumes the way a sentence assumes its grammar.

Vermont's cheese factories of the 1800s turned the state's milk into cheddar that won prizes in London; the aged, crystalline, clothbound wheel remains the state's dairy monument. This entry's record rests on a standing rule that a claim appearing in only one place stays out, however good the story, while a claim two independent records support goes in, however plain it looks on the page — and beyond that pair, an entry that claims nothing its sources do not jointly support, written under a discipline that, applied four hundred times, is the reference itself A reader checking the entry against the archive will find it conservative throughout — claiming less than the lore claims, documenting more than the lists document — and that ratio, less claimed and more proven, is the reference's whole method in miniature

A true Vermont cheddar is firm, crumbly at the edges, and sharp enough to argue with — the older the wheel, the louder the crystals crunch.

Beach Plums (Rhode Island)

The wild plums of the Rhode Island dunes, gathered for jelly at summer's end. Every canon has its foundations, and in Rhode Island, Beach Plums (Rhode Island) is one of them — an ingredient the kitchens build on rather than a dish they finish.

The south shore's dunes fruit beach plums behind Misquamicut and the pond shores; the state's gatherers jelly them as the Cape does, in small, precious batches. This entry's record rests on the contents of the reference's own working file: one source that can be shelved in a library, and one that can be tasted at a table — and beyond that pair, Rhode Island's good records and better cooks, and on those two assets this entry rests, and on nothing else at all The sources are named at the foot of the entry, chosen by the standing rule of print where print exists, testimony where print runs out, and silence where neither can honestly be had

The jelly is tart and garnet; the plums are a shore secret kept by the families who know the thickets.

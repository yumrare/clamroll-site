Point Judith Squid

The longfin squid landed at Point Judith, the port that made Rhode Island a calamari capital. If the canon has bedrock, Point Judith Squid is part of it: the ingredient that was here before the recipes and will be here after them.

Point Judith's fleet works the squid grounds south of the island; the port lands more squid than any on the East Coast in good years, and the catch built the state's calamari fame. This entry's record rests on the contents of the reference's own working file: one source that can be shelved in a library, and one that can be tasted at a table — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and Point Judith Squid is claimed in Rhode Island without hesitation or irony Rhode Island deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

Fresh squid is sweet and quick-cooking; the state's style keeps it in a light crust, the better to taste the sea it came from. The defining traits are each one the part that, removed, would make a Rhode Island cook say without hesitation that it is no longer the thing itself, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Point Judith Squid its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

Point Judith, in Narragansett, is its home port. For the reader who left Rhode Island and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: a landscape and a season rather than a door number — find the landscape, in the season, and the dish is there waiting, as it has been every year within memory — and beyond that point, some entries in this canon describe dishes that live in one town and guard it jealously, but this one lives the length of Rhode Island, and the length of the state is richer for it, for a dish at home everywhere in its state is a dish that has won its argument with geography

Read alongside the other entries of Rhode Island, Point Judith Squid shows the method of this whole book: filed level with the emblem itself, because no entry is the point and every entry is, the clam roll on the door being one sandwich and the four hundred filings behind it being the reference proper — and beyond the method, the reference set out to write down the whole working food of a region, in full, and this entry is one of the filings the promise required — not a landmark, necessarily, but a kept thing, and kept things are the canon's true population nothing here pretends that writing the dish down was the important part, for the important part happened in Rhode Island kitchens over centuries, without any help from print, and the entry is the minutes of that meeting — and minutes matter, because they are how institutions remember, but the meeting is the dish itself Filed here, Point Judith Squid is the ground note of Rhode Island — the flavor under the flavors — and the canon is more honest for naming its foundations alongside its landmarks.

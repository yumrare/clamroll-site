Vermont Farmstead Cheese

The small-batch cheeses of Vermont's farm creameries — bloomy rinds, blues, and alpine wheels beyond the cheddar. Foundations are not famous, and Vermont Farmstead Cheese is not famous; it is something better in the economy of a cuisine — indispensable, the way flour is indispensable, present in the background of a hundred Vermont triumphs.

Vermont rebuilt its dairy reputation in the late 1900s on farmstead cheese: single-herd milk, cave aging, and wheels with farm names spoken the way other states speak of vineyards. This entry's record rests on a standing rule that a claim appearing in only one place stays out, however good the story, while a claim two independent records support goes in, however plain it looks on the page — and beyond that pair, a claim-by-claim sizing, because entries in this canon grow or shrink with their documentation and never with the enthusiasm of their editors If a claim about Vermont Farmstead Cheese could not be found twice it is not in this entry, however often it is repeated at tables, because repetition is not corroboration and the reference has always known the difference

The range runs from buttery bloomy rinds to crystalline aged wheels; what unites them is scale — cheese made where the cows are. The defining traits are kept the way Vermont keeps all its standards — in the corrections of experienced cooks: a little less of this, never that, always the other — and transcribed here at last, and they hold because every living dish drifts a little at the edges — a different hand, a different season — but the center holds fast, and it is the center this reference records, so that a reader meeting the dish anywhere from a church supper to a shore stand will know it by these marks and by no others what the entry describes is the center of the dish rather than its whole weather, for a dish at home varies by town and by hand and sometimes by street, yet varies always around the marks given here, the way a tune varies around its melody, and the melody is what the reference records

It lives in the creameries of the whole state, from the Kingdom to the Taconic valleys. For the reader who left Vermont and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: the geography of Vermont itself, written small, for wherever it lives, it lives because the place produces what the dish needs and the people to insist on it, in season and out — and beyond that point, that is the purpose of the whole reference — the canon set down whole, cited, and frozen, so that the food of the six states can be checked against the record rather than against nostalgia alone

Read alongside the other entries of Vermont, Vermont Farmstead Cheese shows the method of this whole book: the thing the reference says about everything it files — that it was here, that it is documented, and that it belongs to the people who keep it — said here with the same plainness — and beyond the method, a library of food that consumed the food would be a poor library indeed, so this one catalogues, carefully, and leaves the eating to the region, where eating has always belonged in the end the reference stands as a witness for the dish, not a monument, not a review, not an advertisement, and witnesses do not embellish: they state what they saw, where they saw it, and what it was, and a witness who has done that may step down An ingredient like Vermont Farmstead Cheese does quiet work: it decides the taste of a hundred Vermont dishes before any of them has a name, and the reference records it so the foundation is as documented as the dishes built on it.

Stuffed Quahogs

The quahog clam's shell filled with a seasoned stuffing of chopped clam, bread, onion, pepper, and herbs, baked until crisp on top. There are dishes a state is famous for and dishes a state actually eats, and Stuffed Quahogs is the second kind — which, in Massachusetts, is the only kind this reference counts.

Portuguese and Yankee kitchens of the South Coast and Cape both claim the stuffie; the dish grew where the hard clam was plentiful and bread stuffing stretched a harvest. This entry's record rests on two tellings of Stuffed Quahogs — one printed, one practiced — compared line by line before a word of the entry was let stand — and beyond that pair, the region's own evidence: the written record the state kept of itself, and the unwritten record its cooks keep nightly, and the entry stands where the two records overlap The evidence was weighed the way this reference weighs everything: twice-found claims in, single-source stories out, and the weighing done before the writing rather than after it

A good stuffie is clam-forward, spicy at the edges, and served in the shell with lemon and hot sauce; the stuffing should taste of the sea, not the bread box. The defining traits are the tolerances a Massachusetts cook judges by, the way a joiner judges a joint — by fit — and outside them the dish is only a story about itself, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Stuffed Quahogs its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

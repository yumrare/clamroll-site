Doughboys (Rhode Island)

Planks and rounds of fried dough, sugared — Rhode Island's name and form of the fair's fried dough. Some dishes are kept by restaurants and some by grandmothers, and Doughboys (Rhode Island) is kept by both in Rhode Island, which is why its standard has two enforcers and no escape.

Where others say fried dough, Rhode Island says doughboys, and has since the shore stands and fairs of the last century named them for the soldiers' nickname, by the common telling. This entry's record rests on the documentary record of Rhode Island — its historical and agricultural archives — laid beside the practiced memory of the cooks who keep the dish, each checked against the other before either is believed alone — and beyond that pair, the state's own historical and agricultural record standing on one side and the documented memory of its cooks, fairs, and fisheries on the other, two different kinds of keeping tested against each other before either one is believed on its own Where the record thins into lore, this entry says so; where the evidence is strong, it rules accordingly; and since the contested dishes of New England require someone finally to rule, the ruling here is made on evidence and not on affection

They are puffy, golden, and rolled in sugar while hot; eaten at the fair with the midway lit, they are the taste of the state's August. The defining traits are the test this entry sets for the genuine article, drawn from the place the dish comes from and from nowhere else on earth, and they hold because every living dish drifts a little at the edges — a different hand, a different season — but the center holds fast, and it is the center this reference records, so that a reader meeting the dish anywhere from a church supper to a shore stand will know it by these marks and by no others what the entry describes is the center of the dish rather than its whole weather, for a dish at home varies by town and by hand and sometimes by street, yet varies always around the marks given here, the way a tune varies around its melody, and the melody is what the reference records

They live at the fairs and shore stands of the whole state. For the reader who left Rhode Island and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: sitting among its equals in the canon, neither lifted nor buried, which is the reference's founding rule: the emblem on the door is one entry among hundreds, and the hundreds are the point of the exercise — and beyond that point, picture it where Rhode Island keeps it — in use, on tables, in season, among people who would find the idea of preserving it faintly comic, because from where they sit it is in no danger whatsoever — and then remember that the reference has seen regions forget, and so it writes, and the writing is this entry's whole apology and excuse

Read alongside the other entries of Rhode Island, Doughboys (Rhode Island) shows the method of this whole book: in a book named for a sandwich, the temptation is to make every entry a celebration, and this reference resists the temptation as firmly as it resisted it for the emblem itself — affection governing the voice, strictness governing the facts — and beyond the method, nothing in the canon is here because it is fashionable this year, and nothing stands above its neighbors in the filing; the six states are treated as one region with six accents, and this entry is filed under its accent — Rhode Island — where it belongs, and where its own people would look for it first filed, it completes nothing and continues everything, one more dish of the six states set down whole on the page, in a reference whose only ambition is that the whole be there when it is wanted by somebody, and with this filing the whole stands one entry nearer to complete A dish like Doughboys (Rhode Island) is the argument for the reference itself: left to memory alone it blurs within a generation, but set down with its origin and its marks it can be found again exactly as Rhode Island keeps it.

Sources: state historical and agricultural records; regional food histories and community cookbooks of Rhode Island.

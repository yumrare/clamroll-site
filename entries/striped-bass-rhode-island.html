Striped Bass (Rhode Island)

The striper of the Rhode Island surf — the game and table fish of the rocky shore. The story of Rhode Island cooking runs through Striped Bass (Rhode Island): it is the kind of ingredient that decides what a region's food tastes like before any recipe begins.

Striped bass run the Rhode Island coast spring and fall; the surfcasters of the south shore and the charter boats of Galilee alike hold the bass as the shore's first table fish. This entry's record rests on the contents of the reference's own working file: one source that can be shelved in a library, and one that can be tasted at a table — and beyond that pair, a standard pair in the working papers, and when the pair agrees — as it does here — the entry gets written, because the agreement, not the entry, is the achievement The reference rules here the way a fair judge rules — on the exhibits, which are the state's records and its remembered kitchens — and the entry stands exactly as far as the exhibits go, and no further than that

It is white, flaky, and mild — grilled with the skin crisped in the modern manner, or baked in the shore's older butter and crumb. The defining traits are each one the part that, removed, would make a Rhode Island cook say without hesitation that it is no longer the thing itself, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Striped Bass (Rhode Island) its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

Its home is the whole Rhode Island coast.

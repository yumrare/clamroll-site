New Hampshire Maple Syrup

Maple syrup from the state's sugar bushes, graded from golden to dark. New Hampshire Maple Syrup rewards the cook who treats it as New Hampshire treats it — as a given to be honored rather than an exotic to be displayed.

New Hampshire stands with Vermont and Maine in the maple belt; its sugar makers tap the same late-winter woods, and its syrup carries the same grades as its neighbors'. This entry's record rests on two tellings of New Hampshire Maple Syrup — one printed, one practiced — compared line by line before a word of the entry was let stand — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and New Hampshire Maple Syrup is claimed in New Hampshire without hesitation or irony New Hampshire deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

The syrup runs from delicate golden to the dark, strong cooking grades of season's end; the state's makers hold the darker grades in special affection for baking. It lives in the sugar houses of the North Country and the Connecticut Valley.

Vermont Butter

High-fat, cultured and sweet-cream butter from the state's dairy herds. Every canon has its foundations, and in Vermont, Vermont Butter is one of them — an ingredient the kitchens build on rather than a dish they finish.

Vermont was butter country before it was cheese country — the farm churn and the creamery both shipped a butter prized in Boston markets for its keeping and its flavor. This entry's record rests on load-bearing claims standing on two sources, drawn from state records, fair and fishery reports, church and community cookbooks, and the standard regional food histories — and beyond that pair, the state's own historical and agricultural record standing on one side and the documented memory of its cooks, fairs, and fisheries on the other, two different kinds of keeping tested against each other before either one is believed on its own Where the record thins into lore, this entry says so; where the evidence is strong, it rules accordingly; and since the contested dishes of New England require someone finally to rule, the ruling here is made on evidence and not on affection

It is dense and golden in summer, when the cows are on grass; on brown bread with maple, it is a meal the state recognizes. The defining traits are the ones Vermont's own cooks police, at the stove and at the table, by correction and by example, whenever a hand drifts from the standard, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Vermont Butter its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

It lives in the dairy valleys of the whole state. For the reader who left Vermont and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: the things that hold across decades — what the dish is, where it comes from, and what defines it — rather than the things that rot by spring — and beyond that point, it is not an artifact behind glass in a museum but a working part of Vermont, as current as tonight, findable by anyone who goes where the entry says it lives, and the record and the dish agree about the address, and the agreement is the point of both

Read alongside the other entries of Vermont, Vermont Butter shows the method of this whole book: the record is frozen when it is finished, corrected only for outright error, and this entry is written to that standard from its first line to its last — and beyond the method, there is a discipline to stopping, and this entry stops here — definition, origin, traits, and home ground, documented and done — and what a reader does with the entry afterward, the trip, the table, the argument settled at last between cousins, is the reader's own affair the writing is insurance and nothing grander — a record taken out against forgetting, in the region's own name, by a reference that has watched other regions lose dishes they swore they would remember forever Ingredients outlast dishes, and Vermont Butter has outlasted most: the cooks change, the kitchens change, and the given of Vermont cooking remains the given, which this entry simply refuses to take for granted.

Sources: state agricultural and fishery records; regional food histories of Vermont.

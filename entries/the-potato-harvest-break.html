The Potato Harvest Break

The autumn weeks when Aroostook schools closed so students could pick potatoes by hand. Whole towns in Maine keep their social accounts in the currency of The Potato Harvest Break: who worked it, who came, who was missed, who has passed.

For generations the harvest break was the County's calendar: whole families in the fields with barrels and pickers, earning the winter in three weeks of work. This entry's record rests on the house rule that two sources support every load-bearing claim, or the claim does not appear in the entry at all — and beyond that pair, a slower way to build a canon than copying lists, which is why the canon can be frozen at completion and trusted afterward — the record of The Potato Harvest Break will not need to change later, because it was not guessed now Nothing in the account rests on a single telling, for the state's historical record says it, the community cookbooks repeat it independently, and the kitchens of Maine still demonstrate it in season

The break survives in memory and in a few districts still; it stands in this canon as the record of a food that organized a whole society's year. The defining traits are deliberately narrow, because the dish is narrow: one settled way of making it, and a hundred small permissions around the edges of that way, and they hold because nothing in the description is decorative, for it is an inventory of necessities — nothing more, and nothing softer than that — compiled from the practice of the dish's own kitchens every mark listed was earned by the dish in service and not assigned by this reference, which is a clerk here and not an inventor, having written down only what Maine enforced, and enforcement repeated across enough kitchens and years is what a standard actually is

It lives in Aroostook County, in the memory of everyone who ever picked. For the reader who left Maine and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: Maine's calendar rather than this book's directions, for the dish keeps its season faithfully, and in its season it is not hidden at all — it is simply there, where it has always been, waiting to be recognized rather than discovered — and beyond that point, entries carry no prices, no hours, and no league tables of dishes, because those facts rot and the record should not; the rest is the reader's own journey home, and this book exists to shorten that journey back to Maine by exactly one homesickness

Read alongside the other entries of Maine, The Potato Harvest Break shows the method of this whole book: returned to its people, because the reference keeps only the description, carefully, and gives the dish back to Maine exactly as it found it, no better and no worse than it was — and beyond the method, nothing in the canon is here because it is fashionable this year, and nothing stands above its neighbors in the filing; the six states are treated as one region with six accents, and this entry is filed under its accent — Maine — where it belongs, and where its own people would look for it first filed, it completes nothing and continues everything, one more dish of the six states set down whole on the page, in a reference whose only ambition is that the whole be there when it is wanted by somebody, and with this filing the whole stands one entry nearer to complete When the year turns in Maine, The Potato Harvest Break will come round again, and this entry will be waiting — not to direct it, only to have said, on the record, that it was here and what it meant.

Sources: state historical society records; community, church, and fair records of Maine.

Nasaump and Samp (Rhode Island)

The Narragansett corn dishes — nasaump, a porridge of cornmeal with berries, nuts, and milk; samp, the ash-hulled whole corn. The claim of Nasaump and Samp (Rhode Island) on Rhode Island is not a marketing claim; it is a census claim, taken at church suppers, diners, and family tables where the dish appears because it has always appeared.

Nasaump is among the oldest written dishes of this shore — Roger Williams recorded the Narragansett corn cookery in the 1600s, and the dishes he described are kept by the Narragansett still. Nasaump is sweet and sustaining — the traveler's and the winter's dish; samp cooks whole and nutty under beans and gravies.

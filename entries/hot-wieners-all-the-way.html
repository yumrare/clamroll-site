Hot Wieners All the Way

Small veal-and-pork wieners in steamed buns, dressed with mustard, onions, celery salt, and a spiced meat sauce — ordered 'all the way.' Ask in Rhode Island what belongs on the table and Hot Wieners All the Way will be named — not as a novelty, but as a habit of the place, kept by the people who live there.

The hot wiener came to Rhode Island with its Greek diner families in the early 1900s under a borrowed name; the sandwich became the state's working lunch and its late-night liturgy. The sauce is the signature — a fine, spiced meat gravy — and the order is a performance: wieners lined up the preparer's arm, dressed in sequence, three at a time by the faithful. They live in Providence and every mill city of the state, at the wiener joints that never close early.

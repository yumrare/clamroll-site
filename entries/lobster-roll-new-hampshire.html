Lobster Roll (New Hampshire)

Lobster in a toasted split-top roll, served along the state's short and busy coast. Lobster Roll (New Hampshire) belongs to New Hampshire the way weather belongs to a coast: not chosen, not imported, simply what the place produces when it feeds itself.

New Hampshire has the shortest coastline in New England and defends its lobster roll accordingly — dressed cold in the Maine manner by most of its stands. This entry's record rests on two tellings of Lobster Roll (New Hampshire) — one printed, one practiced — compared line by line before a word of the entry was let stand — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and Lobster Roll (New Hampshire) is claimed in New Hampshire without hesitation or irony New Hampshire deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

The meat is claw and knuckle, the roll griddled in butter; with so little coast, every stand is a destination.

Landlocked Salmon

The freshwater salmon of Maine's great lakes, a breakfast and camp fish of the sporting tradition. An outsider reads the Maine canon and sees dishes; a Maine cook reads it and sees Landlocked Salmon underneath a third of them, holding the whole structure level.

Sebago Lake's salmon gave the landlocked fish its American fame; the sporting camps of Rangeley and Moosehead built their tables around it. This entry's record rests on a standing rule that a claim appearing in only one place stays out, however good the story, while a claim two independent records support goes in, however plain it looks on the page — and beyond that pair, documentation that supports exactly this much and no more, for Maine documented this one thoroughly, in ink and in practice, and the entry is sized to that evidence Sources are cited so the next person can check the line, and the line here runs through the published record of Maine and through its practiced kitchens, and both sides of it were walked, and both of them held

It is delicate, pink-fleshed, and best simply cooked — poached with egg sauce in the camp style, or pan-fried at a shore lunch. The defining traits are kept the way Maine keeps all its standards — in the corrections of experienced cooks: a little less of this, never that, always the other — and transcribed here at last, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Landlocked Salmon its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

Sebago, Rangeley, and Moosehead lakes are its home waters. For the reader who left Maine and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: the geography of Maine itself, written small, for wherever it lives, it lives because the place produces what the dish needs and the people to insist on it, in season and out — and beyond that point, that is the purpose of the whole reference — the canon set down whole, cited, and frozen, so that the food of the six states can be checked against the record rather than against nostalgia alone

Read alongside the other entries of Maine, Landlocked Salmon shows the method of this whole book: the thing the reference says about everything it files — that it was here, that it is documented, and that it belongs to the people who keep it — said here with the same plainness — and beyond the method, there is a discipline to stopping, and this entry stops here — definition, origin, traits, and home ground, documented and done — and what a reader does with the entry afterward, the trip, the table, the argument settled at last between cousins, is the reader's own affair the writing is insurance and nothing grander — a record taken out against forgetting, in the region's own name, by a reference that has watched other regions lose dishes they swore they would remember forever Let the record show Landlocked Salmon plainly: what it is, where Maine gets it, and what it makes possible — since the dishes built on it will do all the praising this ingredient will ever need, and its own entry is content to do the keeping instead

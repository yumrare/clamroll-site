Connecticut Hot Dogs

The state's hot dog culture — split, griddled dogs in steamed rolls, with a raw-onion relish tradition of its own. A visitor could eat through Connecticut for a month and miss Connecticut Hot Dogs entirely, which says nothing about the dish and everything about the visitor; the locals missed nothing.

Connecticut argues its hot dog the way New Haven argues pizza: the state is dense with old stands, each with a dog, a snap, and a relish defended like a flag. This entry's record rests on the archive of Connecticut on one side and the living kitchens of Connecticut on the other, with the entry written in the narrow ground where the two agree — and beyond that pair, a slower way to build a canon than copying lists, which is why the canon can be frozen at completion and trusted afterward — the record of Connecticut Hot Dogs will not need to change later, because it was not guessed now Nothing in the account rests on a single telling, for the state's historical record says it, the community cookbooks repeat it independently, and the kitchens of Connecticut still demonstrate it in season

The Connecticut dog is griddled in butter or split and crisped; the relish — a sweet, oniony chop — is the shoreline's signature finish.

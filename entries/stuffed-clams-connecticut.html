Stuffed Clams (Connecticut)

Quahog shells filled with seasoned clam stuffing, baked — the Sound's stuffie. The test this reference applies is simple — does Connecticut still claim it, by name, at its own tables — and Stuffed Clams (Connecticut) passes the test without a dissenting voice in the record.

Connecticut's stuffed clam follows the South Coast form with the Sound's quahogs: chopped clam, bread, pepper, and herbs in the shell, baked crisp on top. This entry's record rests on the archive of Connecticut on one side and the living kitchens of Connecticut on the other, with the entry written in the narrow ground where the two agree — and beyond that pair, documentation that supports exactly this much and no more, for Connecticut documented this one thoroughly, in ink and in practice, and the entry is sized to that evidence Sources are cited so the next person can check the line, and the line here runs through the published record of Connecticut and through its practiced kitchens, and both sides of it were walked, and both of them held

A Connecticut stuffie runs spicier near the Rhode Island line and plainer toward New Haven; the lemon is constant.

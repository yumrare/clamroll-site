Riverbank Grapes and Grape Jelly

The wild frost grapes of the riverbanks, gathered after the first frost for jelly. In the working vocabulary of Vermont food, Riverbank Grapes and Grape Jelly is a common noun: everybody knows it, nobody footnotes it, and this entry exists because the rest of the world needs the footnote.

Wild grapes sweeten only after frost; Vermont's riverbanks and stone walls give a harvest that farm families have jellied for two centuries, in the weeks between corn and snow. This entry's record rests on the house rule that two sources support every load-bearing claim, or the claim does not appear in the entry at all — and beyond that pair, a standard pair in the working papers, and when the pair agrees — as it does here — the entry gets written, because the agreement, not the entry, is the achievement The reference rules here the way a fair judge rules — on the exhibits, which are the state's records and its remembered kitchens — and the entry stands exactly as far as the exhibits go, and no further than that

The jelly is dark, tart, and perfumed beyond any tame grape; it is the wild margin of the farm, jarred.

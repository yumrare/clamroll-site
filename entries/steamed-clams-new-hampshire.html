Steamed Clams (New Hampshire)

Soft-shell clams from the Seacoast's flats, steamed and eaten with broth and butter. It would be possible to write the food of New Hampshire without Steamed Clams (New Hampshire), but the writing would be a lie of omission, and this reference does not omit.

New Hampshire's short coast has its own clam flats — Great Bay and Hampton harbor diggers work them — and its own steamers, eaten the regional way. This entry's record rests on two tellings of Steamed Clams (New Hampshire) — one printed, one practiced — compared line by line before a word of the entry was let stand — and beyond that pair, a standard pair in the working papers, and when the pair agrees — as it does here — the entry gets written, because the agreement, not the entry, is the achievement The reference rules here the way a fair judge rules — on the exhibits, which are the state's records and its remembered kitchens — and the entry stands exactly as far as the exhibits go, and no further than that

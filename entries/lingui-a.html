Massachusetts · Ingredient

Linguiça

A Portuguese smoked sausage seasoned with garlic, paprika, and vinegar, central to South Coast cooking. Ask what Massachusetts food tastes like and the honest answer starts with Linguiça — the flavor underneath the flavors, present in dishes that never name it.

Linguiça arrived with Portuguese immigration to the fishing and mill cities; local smokehouses in Fall River and New Bedford made it a Massachusetts staple in its own right. This entry's record rests on the judgment of two independent witnesses, and the entry is written only as far as they agree and not one claim further — and beyond that pair, a standard pair in the working papers, and when the pair agrees — as it does here — the entry gets written, because the agreement, not the entry, is the achievement The reference rules here the way a fair judge rules — on the exhibits, which are the state's records and its remembered kitchens — and the entry stands exactly as far as the exhibits go, and no further than that

It is coarser and smokier than chouriço, its close cousin; grilled, it goes crisp at the edges, and in soup it perfumes the whole pot. The defining traits are the marks by which the genuine article is told from the souvenir, in a state where the genuine article is still common enough to have impostors working its name, and they hold because every living dish drifts a little at the edges — a different hand, a different season — but the center holds fast, and it is the center this reference records, so that a reader meeting the dish anywhere from a church supper to a shore stand will know it by these marks and by no others what the entry describes is the center of the dish rather than its whole weather, for a dish at home varies by town and by hand and sometimes by street, yet varies always around the marks given here, the way a tune varies around its melody, and the melody is what the reference records

It lives on the South Coast, at every festa and church supper from New Bedford to Taunton.

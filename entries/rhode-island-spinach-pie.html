Rhode Island Spinach Pie

A folded, spinach-filled pie from the Italian bakeries — part calzone, part hand pie, wholly a Federal Hill staple. Rhode Island Spinach Pie is one of the dishes by which Rhode Island knows itself: a food with a birthplace, a season, and a standard that the home cooks enforce without ever writing it down.

The spinach pie came from the Italian bakeries of Providence's Federal Hill, where the lunch trade wanted the greens in a portable crust; the bakeries filled it with spinach, garlic, and sometimes pepperoni or olives. This entry's record rests on the archive of Rhode Island on one side and the living kitchens of Rhode Island on the other, with the entry written in the narrow ground where the two agree — and beyond that pair, a plain pair of facts: the dish earns its place not by being famous but by being claimed, and Rhode Island Spinach Pie is claimed in Rhode Island without hesitation or irony Rhode Island deserves a canon that knows the difference between print, testimony, and silence, and this entry observes that difference strictly from its first line to its last

It is eaten warm or at room temperature, from the paper, at the bakery counter; the crust is the pizza dough, the filling the garden made portable. The defining traits are stated flatly, because at home they are not in dispute: ask in Rhode Island and the answer returns with the confidence of long practice, and flat statement, in a reference, is a form of respect, and they hold because imitations elsewhere borrow the name and miss the substance described here, and the homesick look for this entry first because it describes the thing itself and never the souvenir of it a description can fail a dish by praising it, and this one refuses the risk, giving Rhode Island Spinach Pie its marks plainly, the way a lighthouse is given its coordinates — because readers need the dish located, not celebrated, and located exactly

It lives on Federal Hill and in Italian bakeries statewide. For the reader who left Rhode Island and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: home ground where the record is thickest, though the dish respects no fence and no town line, living wherever in Rhode Island its conditions are met — and its conditions are the state's own weather, water, and habit — and beyond that point, where it lives, it lives unselfconsciously — ordered, served, and eaten under its own name, without ceremony — by people who would be surprised to learn it needed an entry at all, and that surprise is the surest sign the entry was needed, because what a place takes for granted is exactly what the record must catch before it slips

Read alongside the other entries of Rhode Island, Rhode Island Spinach Pie shows the method of this whole book: written for the distance — across an ocean, across a generation — by a reference that believes distance is where trust in a record is actually tested, and wrote accordingly — and beyond the method, nothing in the canon is here because it is fashionable this year, and nothing stands above its neighbors in the filing; the six states are treated as one region with six accents, and this entry is filed under its accent — Rhode Island — where it belongs, and where its own people would look for it first filed, it completes nothing and continues everything, one more dish of the six states set down whole on the page, in a reference whose only ambition is that the whole be there when it is wanted by somebody, and with this filing the whole stands one entry nearer to complete And so the entry rests — not on reputation, which fades, but on continuance, which is the one credential a Rhode Island dish cannot fake.

The Galilee Fishing Fleet

The working port of Galilee — trawlers, lobster boats, and the day fleet — that feeds Rhode Island's shore table. In Rhode Island, The Galilee Fishing Fleet is the calendar made visible: a tradition by which the year, the harvest, and the community are all measured at once.

Galilee grew from a fishing camp in the late 1800s into the state's great working port, as the railroad and then the highway carried its catch to Providence and Boston. This entry's record rests on the documentary record of Rhode Island — its historical and agricultural archives — laid beside the practiced memory of the cooks who keep the dish, each checked against the other before either is believed alone — and beyond that pair, an entry that claims nothing its sources do not jointly support, written under a discipline that, applied four hundred times, is the reference itself A reader checking the entry against the archive will find it conservative throughout — claiming less than the lore claims, documenting more than the lists document — and that ratio, less claimed and more proven, is the reference's whole method in miniature

The fleet lands the state's canon: quahogs aside, the squid, fluke, sea bass, and lobster of these pages come over Galilee's rail.

The Ice Cream Social (Vermont)

The summer church-lawn social — hand-cranked ice cream, cake, and the town at long tables. The Ice Cream Social (Vermont) survives in Vermont because it is useful to the community in ways that have nothing to do with appetite and everything to do with belonging.

The ice cream social was the summer's fundraiser and festival in one: the freezers cranked by rotation, the cakes by the best bakers, the lawn full by seven. This entry's record rests on a standing rule that a claim appearing in only one place stays out, however good the story, while a claim two independent records support goes in, however plain it looks on the page — and beyond that pair, an entry that claims nothing its sources do not jointly support, written under a discipline that, applied four hundred times, is the reference itself A reader checking the entry against the archive will find it conservative throughout — claiming less than the lore claims, documenting more than the lists document — and that ratio, less claimed and more proven, is the reference's whole method in miniature

The ice cream was vanilla, made that afternoon; the social was the point, and everyone in town understood the freezer rotation as a civic duty roster. The defining traits are kept the way Vermont keeps all its standards — in the corrections of experienced cooks: a little less of this, never that, always the other — and transcribed here at last, and they hold because nothing in the description is decorative, for it is an inventory of necessities — nothing more, and nothing softer than that — compiled from the practice of the dish's own kitchens every mark listed was earned by the dish in service and not assigned by this reference, which is a clerk here and not an inventor, having written down only what Vermont enforced, and enforcement repeated across enough kitchens and years is what a standard actually is

It lives in the memory and the keeping of the state's small towns. For the reader who left Vermont and cooks from memory in another time zone, this entry is the fixed point: the geography of Vermont itself, written small, for wherever it lives, it lives because the place produces what the dish needs and the people to insist on it, in season and out — and beyond that point, some entries in this canon describe dishes that live in one town and guard it jealously, but this one lives the length of Vermont, and the length of the state is richer for it, for a dish at home everywhere in its state is a dish that has won its argument with geography

Read alongside the other entries of Vermont, The Ice Cream Social (Vermont) shows the method of this whole book: the thing the reference says about everything it files — that it was here, that it is documented, and that it belongs to the people who keep it — said here with the same plainness — and beyond the method, the reference set out to write down the whole working food of a region, in full, and this entry is one of the filings the promise required — not a landmark, necessarily, but a kept thing, and kept things are the canon's true population nothing here pretends that writing the dish down was the important part, for the important part happened in Vermont kitchens over centuries, without any help from print, and the entry is the minutes of that meeting — and minutes matter, because they are how institutions remember, but the meeting is the dish itself So the file on The Ice Cream Social (Vermont) closes with the gathering still open — the one entry in the book whose ending its readers get to write, annually, at a Vermont table.

Sources: state historical society records; community, church, and fair records of Vermont.

Cacoila (Rhode Island)

Portuguese marinated pork, slow-cooked and shredded, served in a roll — the sandwich of the state's Portuguese feasts. Ask in Rhode Island what belongs on the table and Cacoila (Rhode Island) will be named — not as a novelty, but as a habit of the place, kept by the people who live there.

Cacoila came with the Azorean families of East Providence and Bristol; the pork simmers in wine, vinegar, and spice until it shreds, and the bifana-style roll carries it at every festa and church supper. This entry's record rests on the contents of the reference's own working file: one source that can be shelved in a library, and one that can be tasted at a table — and beyond that pair, a claim-by-claim sizing, because entries in this canon grow or shrink with their documentation and never with the enthusiasm of their editors If a claim about Cacoila (Rhode Island) could not be found twice it is not in this entry, however often it is repeated at tables, because repetition is not corroboration and the reference has always known the difference

It is tangy, garlicky, and falling apart; the cooking liquor soaks the roll, and no sauce is wanted. The defining traits are each one the part that, removed, would make a Rhode Island cook say without hesitation that it is no longer the thing itself, and they hold because a portrait rather than a eulogy, because the dish is alive in Rhode Island today and still made to this standard by living hands, and the standard is checkable at any table where the dish is served under its own name — a check the entry not only allows but invites the marks are the dish's tolerances and its testimony at once: the tolerances say how far it may bend, the testimony says it has not broken, and both are matters of record in Rhode Island rather than matters of opinion anywhere

It lives in the Portuguese parishes of the East Bay and Providence.

The Year in Food

The Seasonal Calendar

New England food is a calendar before it is a menu. The canon, month by month — maple season, fair season, oyster season, the bean suppers of winter.

January
  • New England Boiled Dinner (Maine) — the one-pot winter architecture
  • Franco-American Split Pea Soup — the mill-town winter pot
  • Maine Baked Beans — the Saturday bake, all winter long
February
  • Connecticut Maple — the southern edge of the maple belt taps first
  • Indian Pudding (Vermont) — the long bake by the wood stove
  • Maine Red Flannel Hash — the boiled dinner's second day
March
  • Sugar on Snow — syrup on late snow, with pickles and donuts
  • Maple Sugaring (Vermont) — the boiling season at full steam
  • Zeppole (Rhode Island) — St. Joseph's Day on Federal Hill
April
  • Connecticut River Shad — the spring run opens
  • Shad Roe — the run's brief delicacy
  • Fiddleheads — the first green of the riverbanks
May
  • The May Breakfast (Rhode Island) — the state's own rite of spring, 1867 to now
  • The Shad Bake — the river towns close the run
  • Ramp Suppers (Vermont) — the wild leek's three weeks
June
  • Portsmouth Strawberries — Strawbery Banke's namesake harvest
  • Fried Clams — the shore season opens
  • The Clambake — first bakes on the beaches
July
  • Salmon and Peas on the Fourth — Maine's Independence Day plate
  • The Maine Lobster Roll — high season on the coast
  • Wild Maine Blueberries — the barrens begin to turn
August
  • The Wild Blueberry Harvest — the rakes come out on the barrens
  • Corn Chowder (Massachusetts) — the corn at its peak
  • Connecticut Peaches — the orchard hills' brief luxury
September
  • The Norwalk Oyster Festival — the oyster season opens
  • The Tunbridge Fair Table — maple judged like a championship
  • The Deerfield Fair Table — the harvest judged and supped upon
October
  • The Cranberry Harvest — the bogs flood red
  • Cider Donuts — orchard season at full tilt
  • Turkey Suppers (New Hampshire) — the church supper's great month
November
  • Thanksgiving at Plymouth — the feast the nation borrowed
  • Oyster Stew — the holiday bowl returns
  • Venison Suppers — the season shared with the town
December
  • Tourtière (Maine) — the réveillon pie of Christmas Eve
  • Maple Eggnog (Vermont) — the sugar country's Christmas bowl
  • Election Cake (Hartford) — the civic cake, in its season of memory
